## Introduction

Joining student programs from major tech companies is an excellent way to accelerate your career. In our previous post about [Microsoft Student Ambassadors](/en/posts/microsoft-student-ambassador-2026) (MSA), we discussed how having a public track record of contributions helps build reputation. Now, we will explore the strongest alternatives offered by other industry giants.

In this analysis, our selection criteria wasn't simply listing student benefits or scholarships. We chose initiatives where an undergraduate can contribute publicly: organizing activities, sharing knowledge, building a network, and developing technical leadership. That is what truly makes a difference on a resume.

In 2026, companies like Google, AWS, and GitHub offer programs with these characteristics, allowing students to work in areas like cloud, DevOps, and artificial intelligence. To make your choice and comparison with MSA easier, including also initiatives more focused on growth and advocacy so you can compare the full spectrum of options, we have prepared a quick summary of each one:

| Program                               | Main Focus                              | Applications                   | Similarity to MSA |
| :------------------------------------ | :-------------------------------------- | :----------------------------- | :---------------- |
| **GitHub Campus Experts**             | Community leadership & Open Source      | Annually (July)                | Very high         |
| **GDG on Campus (Google)**            | Broad tech community & Google ecosystem | Varies by region/cycle         | Very high         |
| **Google Student Ambassador 2026**    | Promoting AI and Gemini usage           | Varies by country              | High              |
| **AWS Student Builder Group Leader**  | Technical community for Cloud & AI      | By cohorts / variable calendar | Very high         |
| **AWS Student Builder Campus Leader** | Marketing, cloud adoption & growth      | Varies by campus/region        | Medium            |

_The similarity rating is an editorial assessment considering factors such as community leadership, autonomy to organize events, knowledge sharing, and term continuity._

## GitHub Campus Experts

The **GitHub Campus Experts** program empowers students to build inclusive technical communities. The focus isn't on selling a commercial product, but rather on shaping the student into a strong leader for the Open Source and development ecosystem.

### Benefits

- **Exclusive Training:** A preparatory course focused on empathy, diversity, event organization, and communication.
- **Hackathon Support:** Experts can request logistical support, infrastructure, and swag from GitHub Education to run high-impact events at the university.
- **Networking:** Close contact with other global student leaders and GitHub DevRels. In some editions, highly active students may receive additional opportunities to participate in community initiatives and events.

### Prerequisites and Application

The selection process is highly competitive. Current guidelines and documentation confirm that you must:

1. Be **18 or older** and enrolled in a higher education program.
2. Have **more than one academic year left** before graduating, ensuring you have time to serve.
3. Be a GitHub user for **at least six months**.
4. Have your student status actively validated via the [_GitHub Student Developer Pack_](https://education.github.com/pack).

The application opening is standardized: GitHub typically opens applications every year during the month of **July**, with the application window usually lasting for one month.

The process has two stages: first, you submit the application form, and then you submit a _video resume_ detailing your vision and proposals. Upon final approval, the student undergoes six weeks of training. Only after successfully completing the training does the student officially act as a Campus Expert.

## Google: GDG on Campus and Google Student Ambassador 2026

Currently, Google runs distinct student leadership initiatives. Unlike in the past, when initiatives were centralized mostly around GDSC, today, students have different entry paths depending on their goals.

### Google Developer Groups (GDG) on Campus

GDG on Campus is the historical program, the direct successor to the Google Developer Student Clubs (GDSC). It focuses on developing university communities and covers a broad portfolio of technologies (Google Cloud, Android, AI, Web, etc.).

- **What it is:** The student acts as the local community leader (organizing workshops and hackathons) or as a support member (_Core Team Member_).
- **Benefits:** The official page emphasizes hands-on experience, development of technical and leadership skills, networking, and career preparation. Organizers can also gain recognition for their involvement within the Google Developer ecosystem.
- **How to apply:** Application windows typically vary between regions and cycles; in some editions, the selection of new leaders occurs during the first half of the year. The application requires a detailed form, often involves a leadership profile assessment, and frequently happens through referrals or transitions within existing chapters. Official information is available on the Google for Developers page.

### Google Student Ambassador 2026

This initiative has a strong focus on Gemini and AI. In 2026, there is a Brazilian edition operated by Amplifica in partnership with Google for Education. The program gathers university students for learning, projects, leadership, and knowledge sharing with the academic community. The format, benefits, and selection process may vary significantly between other countries and cycles.

- **What it is:** The ambassador focuses on promoting the Gemini app and educating peers about Google's Generative AI. There may be quantitative goals, such as driving a minimum number of prompt executions or tool adoptions among students.
- **Benefits:** The structure varies depending on the country and cycle. Depending on the edition, participants may receive recognition, rewards, and access to exclusive program activities.
- **How to apply:** Applications for the 2026 cycle already closed in July (with onboarding in August), but the format serves as a reference for what to expect in future editions. Keep an eye on the Google for Education and Amplifica channels for the next cycle. The application window and portal are usually distinct and often independent of the standard GDG calendar.

## AWS: Student Builder Group Leader and Student Builder Campus Leader

AWS underwent a restructuring in 2026, offering two student tracks with very distinct goals and structures:

### AWS Student Builder Group Leader

- **What it is:** The former "AWS Cloud Club Captain." This is the role most focused on the technical community and the closest alternative to MSA. The Group Leader has a 12-month term to lead a study group, organize cloud-focused events, and promote collaborative learning among peers. AWS states the initiative reaches students from over 60 countries, gathering more than 225,000 members globally across community platforms.
- **Benefits:** AWS explicitly confirms that the leader can have access to AWS credits, cloud certification vouchers, swag, and direct support for community activities.
- **Prerequisites and Application:** The process works via a cohort system, with a variable calendar. Group Leaders must be higher education students, and the community is directed towards students aged 18 or older. During selection, AWS typically requires remaining in the institution for at least one year beyond the end of the term, alongside previous experience organizing communities and the ability to demonstrate technical knowledge on video. Since dates and availability may change, we recommend checking the official portal before applying.

### AWS Student Builder Campus Leader

- **What it is:** Another opportunity within the AWS ecosystem, but a role focused on marketing, growth, and adoption. The leader is tasked with increasing student registrations on the AWS Builder Center portal, promoting AWS initiatives on campus, and driving engagement activities (like tabling). Campus Leaders can also have the opportunity to later assume the leadership of an AWS Student Builder Group.
- **Structure and Benefits:** It is a paid part-time role. The contract usually requires 8 to 10 hours a week with a fixed 12-week commitment. Students earn financial compensation for fulfilling program responsibilities, alongside career development and direct access to Amazon teams.
- **Prerequisites and Application:** Currently, AWS itself officially confirms that the program is available in Brazil, the United States, and India. Applications are continuous (rolling), with limited spots per campus.

## Which program is closest to Microsoft Student Ambassadors?

If your goal is to have an experience similar to MSA, focused on building a community, organizing technical events, mentoring other students, and producing valuable content, there are three direct alternatives:

- **GitHub Campus Experts:** Focused on Open Source, it teaches you to build inclusive communities, organize hackathons, and share knowledge, while planning and expanding your impact at the university.
- **GDG on Campus (Google):** You lead the local community and gain practical experience, with high recognition within the Google developer ecosystem.
- **AWS Student Builder Group Leader:** You spend about a year organizing technical activities and building a community, acting as the most faithful counterpart to the technical leadership model.

On the other hand, the **Google Student Ambassador** and the **AWS Student Builder Campus Leader** are also great roles, but they share a different focus: the student acts more on advocacy, awareness campaigns, tool adoption, and growth, with less emphasis on the long-term building of an autonomous technical community.

## Conclusion

These three ecosystems offer different pathways to develop leadership. But the most important thing is not to choose a program just for the company's name or the title on your resume. What truly increases your professional relevance is the public track record you manage to build: events you organized, workshops you taught, articles you wrote, open-source projects you contributed to, and people you helped.

GitHub and AWS, for example, explicitly show that their leaders spend their terms teaching other students, organizing meetups, and building projects practically.

Before applying, carefully read the current documentation for each program and ask yourself: _"Which one will give me the best opportunity to truly contribute and walk away with concrete achievements?"_. The ideal program is the one that will help you transition from someone who "just studies tech" to someone who also produces, teaches, and organizes.
